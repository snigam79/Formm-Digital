Soft knees, knees over toes.

The knee caving in on landing is one of the most studied warning signs for knee injuries, especially in jumping and cutting sports. It’s also very trainable.

The sports science

How we calculate this

Every number in High Performance comes from one of these methods. Thresholds are provisional until our sports-science lead signs them off.

sRPE · session load

Your effort rating (RPE, 0–10) × the minutes you trained, in arbitrary units (AU). A 60-minute session at RPE 6 is 360 AU. Foster et al., 2001.

ACWR · acute:chronic workload ratio

Your last 7 days of load ÷ your weekly average over 28 days, weighted towards recent days (EWMA). Around 0.8–1.3 means you’re training at a level your body is used to; sharp rises above that are the pattern to avoid. Researchers debate how well ACWR predicts injury, so we use it as one signal, never on its own. Gabbett, 2016; Williams et al., 2017; Impellizzeri et al., 2020.

Monotony and strain

Monotony = average daily load ÷ its standard deviation across the week. Strain = weekly load × monotony. High monotony (above about 2) means every day is equally hard, which has been linked to illness and staleness. Foster, 1998.

Wellness score

Sleep, fatigue, stress and muscle soreness, each 1–7, added up. A score that rises well above your own normal is an early sign you’re not recovering. Hooper and Mackinnon, 1995.

Left–right balance

From your Formm sessions: the weaker side’s range as a percentage of the stronger side (limb symmetry). Physios often look for 90% or more before a return to full training.

Sleep

Hours slept, from your wearable, against your own 30-day normal. Short sleep is one of the most consistent factors linked to injuries in young athletes. Milewski et al., 2014.

HRV · heart rate variability

From your wearable’s overnight readings. We take the natural log of the reading, average the last 7 days, and compare it with a normal band built from your own last 60 days. Several days below the band suggests you’re not absorbing training. Different watches measure HRV differently (rMSSD or SDNN), so we never compare across devices or with other people. Plews et al., 2013; Buchheit, 2014.

Resting heart rate

A resting heart rate several beats above your own normal, for more than a day or two, often comes with poor recovery or illness.

TRIMP · heart-rate training load

Minutes spent in each heart-rate zone, weighted by zone. Used to check the effort you logged, never to replace it. Banister, 1991; Edwards, 1993.

Gait

Walking asymmetry, speed, step length and double-support time come from iPhones (Apple Health). Running cadence, ground contact time and left–right balance come from Garmin running dynamics. We look for changes from your own normal, and for several signals pointing at the same side of the body.

Jump height · countermovement jump

From flight time: height = g × t² ÷ 8, where t is the time from take-off to landing in the slow-motion video. Compared with your own baseline, a drop in jump height is one of the most sensitive early signs of neuromuscular fatigue. Balsalobre-Fernández et al., 2015; Claudino et al., 2017.

Landing quality

We check the landing for the errors used in the Landing Error Scoring System: knees caving in, stiff landings, uneven feet and trunk lean. Knees caving in on landing has been linked to knee ligament injury. Padua et al., 2009; Hewett et al., 2005.

Single-leg hop symmetry

Hop distance on the weaker leg as a percentage of the stronger leg. 90% or more is a common return-to-sport benchmark.

Pain 0–10

A pain-monitoring approach used in rehab: low pain that settles by the next morning is usually acceptable; pain that is high, sharp or worse the next day means stop and get it checked. Silbernagel et al., 2007. Formm never diagnoses pain.
